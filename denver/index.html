About this map and your privacy

Privacy: This page has no backend, no analytics, and no cookies, and nothing you type is stored by crashmaps. Fonts and data come from crashmaps.org itself. Loading the map and charts fetches scripts from cdnjs (Cloudflare) and map tiles from CARTO, and each of those services sees your IP address. An address you search is sent to OpenStreetMap Nominatim, and commute start and end points are sent to the public OSRM routing server. Those requests are subject to each service’s privacy policy.

14 — Methodology

How I Built This

Underreporting: Only crashes with a police report appear here. Many crashes, especially those involving pedestrians and cyclists, are never reported, so the true numbers are higher.
Scooters and e-bikes: CDOT records e-scooter riders as their own non-motorist type (“Scooter”), separate from pedestrians, along with other personal conveyances; both are counted here as scooters and other personal devices. E-bikes have no separate code and are counted with bicycles.
Data pipeline: CDOT Statewide Crash Listing Excel files parsed via Python adapter. Records filtered to the six metro counties (Adams, Arapahoe, Broomfield, Denver, Douglas, Jefferson). 49,021 records for 2025.
City assignment: The CDOT City field maps crashes to 17 metro city groups. When City is blank (common on sheriff and State Patrol reports), the city is assigned from the crash coordinates using official DOLA municipal boundaries; crashes outside every municipality go to their county’s catch-all city group.
Behavioral flags: DUI from alcohol/marijuana/drug suspected fields. Speed from contributing factor text and speed-over-limit detection (>10 mph over). Hit-and-run from TU hit-and-run boolean fields. Pedestrian and cyclist from crash type text.
Hotspots: Each hotspot counts every crash within 300 m of its busiest point, so a freeway interchange includes its ramps and can span up to about 600 m. 2 of the 65 hotspots on this page come from records that give a street with no cross street. Those are grouped by 200-metre square and labelled “[street] near [nearest cross street]”, with the cross street taken from OpenStreetMap. The count covers every crash in the square, including crashes on the other streets inside it. Squares next to each other are listed separately.
