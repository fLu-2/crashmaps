What this is

I turn official crash records from state and city transportation agencies into interactive dashboards for 20 US metros. Every figure traces back to a public dataset, linked from each dashboard's Sources section.

Each dashboard maps crash hotspots and breaks crashes down by timing, severity, impairment, speed, hit-and-run, and pedestrian and cyclist safety. Its Methodology section explains how the data was processed.

The dashboards show summaries. Individual crash records stay with the agencies that publish them, and the Sources links lead there.

I built crashmaps so the people who drive, walk, and bike these roads can see what happens on them. If your city publishes crash records, use the form to request it.

If you find the dashboards useful, you can buy me a coffee.

No affiliation with any law firm, insurance company, or government agency. No ads, analytics, or cookies.

Can I download the raw data?

The dashboards show summaries only. Each dashboard's Sources section (for example, Denver's) links to the agency dataset where the full records are published.

Can you add my city?

Possibly, if its city or state publishes crash records with locations through at least 2024. Send a "Request a city" message above; some states restrict access to crash data, so not every city can be added.

How is the data processed?

Each agency's records are mapped to a common schema, aggregated, and built into a standalone page. Each dashboard's Methodology section (for example, Denver's) explains that source's quirks and exclusions.
